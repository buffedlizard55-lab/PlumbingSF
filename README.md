# Verified San Francisco Plumbers — one specific job

A reproducible research system and static site for a narrow plumbing problem:

> Find a currently licensed plumber who can clear a slow shower and bathtub drain, then safely
> remove a stuck bathtub overflow trip-lever assembly in a c.1940 San Francisco rental **without
> opening walls, replacing concealed piping, or accessing the tub from below**.

**Published URL:** <https://buffedlizard55-lab.github.io/PlumbingSF/>

The site is generated from source captures and structured curation; it requires no manual data entry
at runtime.

## Most important conclusion

**No retained source proves that any listed contractor has safely extracted a seized c.1940
trip-lever linkage under the exact no-access constraint.** Generic “bathtub repair” categories are
not treated as proof.

The three first-screen leads are:

1. **ASAP Plumbing Pros** — active CSLB 1113916; a current direct Thumbtack review specifically
   describes clearing a shower drain that other plumbers hesitated to tackle. The platform's employee
   count conflicts with two CSLB workers-comp exemptions.
2. **Atlas Plumbing & Rooter** — active CSLB 868994; direct BBB evidence for successful adjacent
   drain snaking plus advertised shower/bathtub and camera scope.
3. **Costello's Plumbing** — active CSLB 1018923; direct Thumbtack condo/apartment drain-clearing
   review and advertised shower/bathtub repair. Confirm the workers-comp classification anomaly.

They are leads to **screen**, not confirmed overflow specialists. Ask:

> Have you removed a seized trip-lever linkage through the overflow opening on an old tub? Will you
> snake the shower and stop before opening a wall or replacing concealed piping unless the landlord
> separately authorizes that work in writing?

## Source access and review policy

| Source | Access in this pass | How it is used |
|---|---|---|
| CSLB License Detail | Direct | Decisive license status, legal name, classification, bond, WC, dates, reissue and disclosure text |
| SF Open Data | Direct stored extract | Permit-activity corroboration only; never proof of current authority |
| Thumbtack | Direct for current selected profiles | Ratings, service menus and limited named review text |
| Expertise.com | Direct | Provider descriptions and explicitly **indirect-aggregator** Yelp/Google/Thumbtack totals |
| Yelp | Direct fetch blocked (HTTP 403) | Indexed search snippets only, badged `indirect-search-snippet`, plus live links for manual review |
| Google Maps | No stable review payload | No direct Google quotation; manual links and labelled Expertise aggregates only |
| Reddit | Direct fetch blocked (HTTP 403) | No candidate-specific Reddit quotation is published |
| BBB | Direct inherited captures | Business-profile service and accreditation text |
| Oatey/Dearborn and Gerber | Direct | Manufacturer mechanism/access guidance, not contractor qualification |

Ratings are snapshots, not scoring inputs for licensure. Every source block records its URL, capture
date, access method, and trust tier.

```text
data/raw/cslb-*.txt
        │
        └── scripts/extract_cslb.py ──► data/_cslb_extract.json
                                              │
data/curation.json ───────────────────────────┤

                                              ▼
                                    scripts/build_data.py
                                              │
                             data/plumbers.json + master_list.csv
                                              │
                                    scripts/validate.py
                                  (non-zero exit blocks publish)
                                              │
                                    scripts/build_site.py
                                              ▼
                                       docs/index.html
```

The validator enforces:

1. every cited raw file exists and every source URL uses a visited allow-listed host;
2. every primary license has an official `cslb-<license>.txt` capture;
3. all copied CSLB fields match the parsed capture, including status text, entity, dates, reissue,
   classifications, address, phone, bond, workers compensation and miscellaneous information;
4. active/suspended/expired status codes agree with literal CSLB wording;
5. every published excerpt and every score/count occurs in the matching URL's source block;
6. item-level access labels agree with capture access, including the explicitly modelled case of a
   directly fetched aggregator displaying an indirect platform rating;
7. only active licenses can appear in recommended/viable/conditional tiers;
8. IDs and primary licenses are unique;
9. each expansion pass has exactly its locked entry count (20 and 50) and exactly the expected
    license numbers;
10. expansion licenses do not overlap any pre-existing primary or sibling license, and normalized
    expansion names do not duplicate pre-existing names;
11. the expansion status summaries remain 17/2/1 (20-entry pass) and 31 active, 9 expired,
    8 canceled, 2 suspended (50-entry pass);
12. required irregularity flags remain attached to every affected expansion license (7 and 19);
13. every entry's published SF permit count equals the strict sum of its primary and sibling
    license rows in the SFGov permit extracts.

## Permit and rental context

The site reproduces and links the source text rather than substituting legal advice.

- San Francisco Plumbing Code / Building Code §104.2 exempts unstopping traps, sewers, vents or
  waste pipes when the work does not require cutting into or removing traps or piping.
- California Civil Code §1941.1 requires plumbing facilities maintained in good working order. The
  slow drains are a condition to document in writing; whether a legal threshold is met depends on
  severity and facts.
- The site links the San Francisco Tenants Union repair process and carries its warning about calling
  DBI from an unpermitted unit.
- This research is not legal advice. The tenant should not personally authorize demolition or
  concealed-pipe replacement outside the landlord's written scope.

## Caveats

- License status, bonds, workers-comp policies and ratings change. Re-check the linked live pages.
- A platform's “licensed” badge is not current contracting authority; CSLB controls.
- Permit counts prove historical filing activity, not drain skill, customer satisfaction or current
  authority. Drain clearing may also be permit-exempt.
- Service-menu scope is weaker than a relevant customer account, and adjacent drain work is weaker
  than proof of this exact seized linkage.
- `recommended` means **first-screen lead**, not endorsement or guaranteed workmanship.
- The page is research, not legal advice.
